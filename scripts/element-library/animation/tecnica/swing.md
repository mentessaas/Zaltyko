# Technique reference — swing apparatus (HB, UB, PB, Rings)

Animator's reference for how coaches teach the swinging families. The facts are written in our
own words, and the sources are listed at the end with [n] references. Values marked **(est.)** are
approximate: they come from pendulum physics, apparatus sizes or common coaching practice, not
from a measured study. Use them as plausible defaults and do not quote them as data.

## 0. Conventions used in this file

- **Joint angles** (anatomical, not screen):
  - **Shoulder angle** = arm–trunk angle. 0° = arms down by the sides, 180° = arms straight
    overhead in line with the trunk, >180° = "open/hyperflexed" (arms behind the head line),
    <180° = "closed" (arms pulled toward the hips). Shoulder closing on a bar = **shoulder
    extension**, the main pulling action.
  - **Hip angle** = trunk–thigh. 180° = straight, <180° = pike/flexion, >180° = arch/hyperextension.
  - **Knee/elbow** 180° = straight. On every swing element, straight arms and straight legs are the
    norm. A bent arm or knee is an execution error unless the skill is a tuck.
- **Position angle φ around the bar** (for circles): how far the centre of mass (CoM) has travelled
  from the handstand. 0° = handstand directly above the bar, 90° = body horizontal on the way
  down, 180° = straight below the bar (bottom), 270° = horizontal on the way up, 360° = handstand.
  To map to screen (ANIMATOR.md: 0 = up, clockwise +), a backward giant (backward-salto rotation
  sense) runs counter-clockwise for a figure with `facing` +1, so it is a negative rotation.
- **Body shapes**: *straight* (hip 180°, shoulders 180°); *hollow/dish* (hip ≈165–175°, ribs in,
  pelvis tucked, shoulders ≈170–180°); *arch* (hip ≈190–205°, shoulders ≈190–200°); *pike*
  (hip < 120°); *tuck* (hip and knee < 90°).
- **Apparatus heights (FIG norms, approximate, from the floor)**: HB 2.80 m (bar Ø 28 mm, elastic,
  deflects visibly at the bottom of giants); UB high bar 2.50 m, low bar 1.70 m, bars ≈1.3–1.8 m
  apart diagonally; PB 2.00 m, rails ≈42–52 cm apart; Rings 2.80 m (rings hang ≈0.5 m apart on
  ≈3 m straps). Our scene anchors already use these values.

## 1. The universal swing mechanics (read first; every family below reuses it)

**The pendulum pump ("tap", "beat", "kip action")**: a gymnast cannot add energy at a
fixed length by staying rigid. Energy is added by changing the hip and shoulder angles at the right
moment: **arch (lengthen) into the bottom, then close hips and shoulders (shorten/flex) just after
the bottom, and extend again in the upper part of the circle** [4][7]. The biomechanics literature
names the window under the bar, from maximum hip hyperextension to hip flexion and from maximum
shoulder hyperflexion to shoulder extension, the **functional phase**. About 70 % of the
gymnast's muscular work is done in it [7][8]. The hip is the main generator, with the shoulder
second [9][10].

Coach language for the same thing:
- "Stretch long down the front, arch through the bottom, **tap** (kick) the toes forward/up, close
  the shoulders (pull the bar toward the thighs), stay hollow on the way up, open to handstand."
- "Lead with the chest on the way down, lead with the toes on the way up" (forward swing in hang).
- "Back leads, heels follow" on back swings in support (PB) and on rings. Heels leading with an
  arch is the classic error [1].

**Timing (generic swing, as a fraction of the circle or swing)** (est. from [4][7]):

| Phase | φ (giant) | Hip | Shoulder | Body | Coach cue |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Drop from handstand | 0→≈100° | 175–180 (slight hollow) | 180 | Straight or hollow, head neutral | "Stay tall, fall long" |
| Stretch/arch into bottom | ≈120→170° | opens to ≈195–205 | opens to ≈190–200 | Long arch, feet trailing | "Reach, stretch, chest leads" |
| Tap / beat (functional phase) | ≈170→230° | snaps to ≈150–165 | closes to ≈150–165 | Hollow, toes lead upward | "Tap! Kick the toes up, pull the bar down" |
| Rise | ≈230→320° | stays ≈165–175 | ≈160–175 | Hollow | "Stay hollow, keep the bar pushed away" |
| Open to handstand | ≈320→360° | 180 | 180 | Straight handstand, shoulders open | "Push tall, finish over the bar" |

The tap takes only ≈0.15–0.25 s and is fast and short. The animator must show it as a quick snap
from arch to hollow just past vertical, not as a slow bend.

**Speed and time (est.)**: CoM radius in hang ≈1.0–1.2 m from the bar. The CoM drops ≈2.2 m from
handstand to the bottom, so the CoM speed at the bottom is ≈6–7 m/s (≈6 rad/s, ≈340°/s). It is
slowest at the top (≈1–2 rad/s). One full giant takes **≈1.2–1.6 s**, with about 45 % of the time
spent in the upper half because rotation is slow there. Bar reaction at the bottom is a few body
weights (≈3–5 BW, higher in accelerated giants). The HB visibly bends down a few cm at the bottom
and springs back on the upswing.

## 2. HIGH BAR (MAG)

### 2.1 Long hang swing (basic swing) and the tap swing
- **Start**: cast out from support or jump to hang. Overgrip, arms straight, shoulders fully open.
- **Forward swing** (front of body leading on the way down): body long and slightly arched coming
  into the bottom. Just past the bottom: **tap**, a fast hip flexion and shoulder closing so the toes
  "kick" forward-up. The body then rises in a hollow shape. At the end of the forward swing, hips
  are around bar height in a slight dish (shoulders 150–170°).
- **Back swing**: a small re-arch passing the bottom, then hollow. The back swing ends in a hollow
  body above horizontal (≥45° in FIG Age Group tests) [1][2]. Hands **shift (regrip)** at the end of
  each forward swing so the grip stays on top of the bar.
- **Errors**: piking or "kicking" early before the bottom (energy lost); bent arms; a loose banana
  arch on the upswing; no regrip; legs apart; head pushed forward or thrown back.
- **Amplitude progression**: swing below horizontal → to horizontal → 45° → handstand-height
  swings. Irwin & Kerwin rank progressions by how closely their functional phase matches the real
  longswing [7].

### 2.2 Backward giant (overgrip) and grips
- Use the generic table in §1. Key details:
  - Passing φ ≈ 0–90°: body straight, slightly hollow, eyes toward the hands, shoulders open.
  - φ ≈ 120–170°: stretch into a mild arch (the "lengthening" before the bottom).
  - φ ≈ 170–230°: the tap. Hips flex ≈30–45° and shoulders close ≈20–40°, so the feet "lead" up the
    back side.
  - φ ≈ 270–360°: re-extension. Shoulders open and hips extend back to a straight line. A late
    re-grip shift of the hands happens around φ ≈ 300–340° (the hands rotate over the bar).
  - FIG expectation: **minimal pike and minimal arm–trunk angle change, passing through a fully
    extended handstand** [1][2].
- **Forward giant (undergrip)**: the mirror image. The gymnast falls back-first, so the downswing
  is back-leading and the bottom swing is a back swing in hang. Use a **hollow** downswing (never an
  arch on the back-leading half), a slight arch and then a hollow/"heel drive" past the bottom, and
  close the shoulders as the body climbs. Undergrip giants look slightly more piked going over the
  top.
- **El-grip (eagle) giants**: forward-direction giants with the arms rotated inward and behind
  (dorsal shoulder position). The shoulders stay very open (≥200°), and the body is usually slightly
  piked through the top. They need extreme shoulder flexibility.
- **Mixed grip**: transitional; seen after ½ turns.
- **Accelerated giants** (the giant before a dismount or release): a bigger arch before the bottom
  and a sharper, bigger tap. Elite gymnasts use two distinct accelerated-giant patterns
  ("traditional" and "scooped"). They differ in when the hips and shoulders flex on the downswing.
  At Sydney 2000, 95 % used the scooped pattern, which gives a wider release window [5][6].
  For animation: deepen the arch and the tap, and shorten the duration by ≈10 % (est.).

### 2.3 Circles close to the bar: Stalder, Endo, Adler
All start and end in handstand. The defining feature is that the body circles **close to the bar
in a deep pike**, with legs straddled (Stalder, Endo) or together (stoop, Adler).
- **Stalder (backward, overgrip, straddled)**:
  1. Fall from handstand with a straight body to φ ≈ 30–60°.
  2. **Straddle-in**: hips flex fast and legs open wide so the feet pass outside the hands. The
     shoulders open slightly so the hips stay high over the bar. Coaches want the straddle-in a
     little **delayed** (an early straddle-in is a WAG execution error) [2]. Elite gymnasts flex
     the hips before the shoulders begin to extend [14].
  3. Pass under the bar in **maximum pike** (hip ≈30–45°, chest to thighs, shoulder angle ≈150–170°,
     arms straight). The CoM is very close to the bar, so the circle is fast here.
  4. **Straddle-out**: on the rising side (φ ≈ 250–300°), extend the hips and close the legs
     continuously while pushing the shoulders open, ending in a hollow then straight handstand.
     The straddle-out is what separates better from weaker gymnasts [14]. Hip flexibility strongly
     changes the required joint torques [13].
  - **Errors**: arching during the opening, insufficient pike at the bottom, legs touching the bar,
    bent arms, a late opening that stalls short of handstand.
- **Endo (forward, undergrip, straddled)**: the mirror of the Stalder. Fall back-first, straddle-in
  past horizontal with the back leading, hold the deep pike under the bar, then straddle-out to
  handstand.
- **Stoop circles and Adler**: legs together, feet between the hands (stoop-in). The Adler is the
  el-grip stoop circle to handstand (core of FIG HB element group IV [3]). It needs extreme pike
  and shoulder flexibility. Gymnasts are classified by whether hip and shoulder are opened high
  (>160°) as the CoM passes over the bar [25].
- **Timing (est.)**: about 1.3–1.7 s per circle. The bottom half is faster than in a giant
  (smaller radius) and the top is slow. Keys: handstand (0) → straddle/stoop-in (0.25) → max pike
  at bottom (0.5) → straddle-out start (0.7) → handstand (1.0).

### 2.4 Pirouettes in handstand
Giant with ½ turn (to mixed or undergrip), with 1/1 turn (full pirouette), and Stalder/Endo/Adler
with turns.
- The turn **starts before the handstand is reached**, on the upswing at φ ≈ 300–330°. One hand
  releases, the body turns about the support arm, and the free hand regrasps in the new grip.
  The FIG rule is that the turn must begin before the giant is completed and finish in an extended
  handstand [1][2]. Turn with **one hand at a time** [1][2].
- Body straight to slightly hollow, shoulders fully open, the head stays between the arms. The turn
  axis is the support arm, so the body leans onto it.
- **Errors**: late turn (after the vertical), arch, piking, a hop (both hands off) when the element
  is not a hop turn, regrasp off-line (the body ends angled to the bar).
- **Timing**: the ½ turn takes ≈0.2–0.3 s and the 1/1 turn ≈0.4–0.6 s (est.). A full pirouette
  needs a regrip of the first hand halfway through.

### 2.5 Release moves (release and regrasp)
General rule: the release is the end of a well-timed tap. The gymnast lets go when the CoM
velocity points where the skill needs it, and the flight is ballistic (ANIMATOR.md `flight`). The
release instant matters to within tens of milliseconds [6][12]. The CoM rises ≈0.5–1.0 m above
the bar; flight time is ≈0.7–1.0 s (est.).

- **Tkatchev family (counter-straddle backward over the bar)**: entered from a backward giant.
  1. Drop down the front with a big arch.
  2. Pass the bottom and tap.
  3. On the rising side, **close the shoulders hard and keep or deepen the hip pike** so the legs
     drive up while the shoulders press down and away. This "reverse" action reduces the backward
     rotation, giving the counter-movement. Gervais & Tally called this preparatory hip flexion the
     **beat swing** and linked it to the release determinants [11].
  4. Release on the upswing well before the handstand (est. φ ≈ 270–310°). The body travels up and
     back over the bar with the back to the bar, straddling high (legs ≥90° apart) with the chest
     facing down toward the bar.
  5. Catch the bar while descending on the far side (the side where the giant's downswing began),
     with the arms reaching forward for the bar. The swing continues downward.
  - Variants: straddle (core) → piked → stretched (layout) → with ½/1/1 turn; the Moznik regrasps in
    el-grip [3]. Consistency studies show the release window is narrow [12].
  - **Errors**: release too late (body over-rotates and is close to the bar), too early (no height),
    bent arms at the catch, legs low or under-straddled, the catch below bar height.
- **Gienger (backward salto piked/stretched with ½ turn, regrasp on the same side)**: from a backward
  giant. Release on the rising side near horizontal, as for a flyaway but with more upward and less
  away velocity. Do a back salto with a ½ twist **started early** so the gymnast faces the bar. The
  hands catch in front, in hang. Kolman-type twisting is the stretched version.
- **Kovacs (double salto backward over the bar, tucked/piked/stretched; Kolman = Kovacs with 1/1
  twist, Cassina = stretched with twist)**: from an accelerated backward giant.
  1. Big tap.
  2. Release **later than a flyaway**, around or past horizontal on the rising side, so the CoM
     flies up and **back over the bar**.
  3. Double back salto above the bar (the CoM peaks ≈1 m+ above the bar, est.). Kick out and reach
     for the bar on the far side.
  - **Errors**: release too late (the head hits the bar), opening late, catch with bent arms.
- **Jäger family (front salto over/in front of the bar)**: from forward giants (undergrip) or a
  forward swing in el or mixed grip. On the **back swing** (back-leading rise), hips arch and then
  pike, and the gymnast releases as the heels drive up, doing a forward salto (tucked, piked,
  straddled or stretched) and regrasping in front of the bar in undergrip or mixed grip.
  - **Deltchev**: from a backward giant, a ½ twist early in flight turns the backward rotation into
    a front-salto look, and the gymnast travels **over** the bar to regrasp.
  - **Errors**: releasing early (the gymnast lands far from the bar), insufficient rotation, a piked
    catch with the knees hitting the bar.

### 2.6 Dismounts (flyaways, double/triple saltos with twists)
- **Entry**: 1–2 accelerated giants. Each passes the bottom with a big arch and then a strong tap.
- **Release**: on the rising side, **around horizontal** (est. φ ≈ 240–280° for doubles; triples
  release a little later and at a higher velocity). Shoulders and hips are opening at release. The
  hands push the bar away. The release window lasts only a few tens of ms, and the "scooped"
  giant widens it [6]. Maximal-dismount simulations show where the trade-off between rotation and
  height lies [24]. On UB dismounts at the 1994 World Championships, the shoulder angle at release
  averaged ≈144° and the hip angle ≈148° [20].
- **Flight**: the CoM rises ≈1.0–1.6 m above the release point (ANIMATOR.md), flight ≈1.0–1.3 s
  (est.).
  - Layout flyaway: slightly hollow throughout; FIG says "slightly hollow, whole body above the
    bar" [1].
  - Double tuck: tuck quickly after release, rotate in a tight tuck, **kick out** (open) by about
    the last ¼ turn, and land with the arms up.
  - Twisting dismounts: twisting starts in the first salto (e.g. full-in) or the last (full-out).
    Twist in a straight body or with arms wrapped. The arms close in to the chest during the twist
    and open to stop it before landing.
- **Landing**: feet slightly in front of the CoM, knees and hips absorb (knee ≈110–130°, hip
  ≈100–130°), arms forward-up, then stand up.
- **Errors**: early release (the gymnast flies far and low), late release (flying back toward the
  bar), arching in a layout, tucking late, a low dismount (CoM below bar height), not opening before
  landing.

### 2.7 Animation recipe — High bar
Use a contact phase (hands pinned to `bar`) with keys at φ values, and a flight phase for releases
and dismounts.
1. **Giant (backward)**, total ≈1.4 s. Keys:
   - φ0 handstand: straight, shoulders 180, hip 180.
   - φ90 (0.20): straight to hollow.
   - φ150 (0.38): arch (hip 200, shoulder 195).
   - φ195 (0.50): tap, start of the snap (hip 175).
   - φ225 (0.56): hollow (hip 155, shoulder 160), toes leading.
   - φ300 (0.78): hollow (hip 170, shoulder 170).
   - φ360 (1.0): straight handstand.
   - The bar dips a few cm at φ ≈ 180–200.
2. **Long hang swing**: forward end (hips around bar height, hollow) → bottom (slight arch) → just
   past bottom (tap) → back end (hollow above horizontal) → regrip shift at the forward end.
   ≈1.0–1.2 s per half swing (est.).
3. **Stalder/Endo**: handstand → straight fall (0.15) → straddle-in, hips at 90 (0.3) → max pike
   with the chest on the thighs under the bar (0.5) → straddle-out begins (0.7) → hollow (0.85) →
   handstand (1.0). Keep the legs straight and wide, the feet never touching the bar.
4. **Release**: last contact key = the release pose with a correct velocity direction:
   - Tkatchev: body ≈45° past bottom on the rising side, hips piked ≈140, shoulders closed ≈140.
   - Kovacs: body past horizontal, opening.
   - Flight shapes: Tkatchev = straddle, chest down. Kovacs = tuck by u 0.25, open at u 0.85.
   - Regrasp contact begins with the arms reaching overhead toward the bar.
5. **Dismount**: accelerated giant (arch deeper, tap sharper) → release at ≈horizontal on the
   upswing → flight with shapes (tuck/pike/layout; twist window inside the salto) → landing
   absorption.
6. **Poster**: for giants, the tap frame (hollow, toes leading) plus ghosts at handstand and at
   the arch. For releases, the peak of flight above the bar.

## 3. UNEVEN BARS (WAG)
The swing mechanics are the same as HB (§1). WAG-specific differences: the low bar is in the way
of the long hang swing (bars ≈1.3–1.8 m apart), so swings use a **tap swing** that passes in a
hollow or arch shape that clears the low bar. Transitions between bars are core content. Bars are
thicker and less elastic than the HB.

### 3.1 Glide kip (upstart)
1. **Jump and glide**: from a stand (or a small run), jump to grip the low bar. Pike, with the hips
   behind the bar, then glide forward with the feet just above the mat (≈10–30 cm).
2. **Extend at the end of the glide**: hips open to nearly straight, the body long, shoulders open
   ≈180°. The FIG test wants an "extended jump with hips at bar height" for MAG [1].
3. **Ankles to bar**: at the end of the glide (as the swing stops forward), pike fast and bring the
   ankles to the bar (hip ≈30–40°).
4. **Kip pull** during the back swing: keep the ankles near the bar and "**slide the bar up the
   legs**" ("pull up your trousers"). Arms straight, shoulders close strongly from ≈180° to
   ≈30–45°.
5. **Finish**: front support with the hips on the bar, shoulders over the bar, body slightly hollow,
   arms straight. The FIG test wants the hips at least at elbow height at the end [1].
- Timing: glide ≈0.6–0.8 s, ankles-to-bar ≈0.2 s, kip pull ≈0.4–0.6 s (est.). The timing of the
  kip action matters more than strength, and elite technique favours **robustness to timing
  error** over minimum effort [12].
- **Errors**: bent arms, legs dropping away from the bar, kipping too early (before the end of the
  glide), insufficient glide extension, finishing with the hips below the bar or arched.

### 3.2 Cast to handstand
1. Front support: arms straight, shoulders slightly in front of the bar, body hollow.
2. A small pike (legs swing under/forward) as a **pre-swing** (≈0.2 s).
3. Explosive hip extension backward-upward while the shoulders open and **lean forward over the
   bar**, with straight arms pressing down on the bar. Body hollow; can be straddled or closed.
4. Rise to handstand with the shoulders directly over the hands.
- FIG expectations: casts to horizontal with no pike/arch; cast handstand with straight arms; the
  cast may be done "chin-up" (bent arm) or "beat swing" (straight arm) style at the early levels
  [1][2].
- **Errors**: arching, bent arms, too little shoulder lean (falls back) or too much (overshoots), a
  piked handstand.

### 3.3 Clear hip circle (free hip) to handstand
1. From a cast (as high as possible), fall back: **hips stay close to the bar without touching**,
   body hollow, shoulders lean back behind the bar.
2. Under the bar: a hollow shape with the shoulders closing slightly (pulling the bar toward the
   hips) and toes leading the circle.
3. Rising side: open the shoulders strongly ("push the bar away"), extend the hips and finish in
   handstand.
- WAG tests: clear hip "above 45°" → to handstand [2].
- **Errors**: hips touching the bar, a piked body, an early shoulder opening (body falls away),
  bent arms, finishing short.

### 3.4 Stalder, toe-on and pike sole circles
- **Stalder**: as for HB (§2.3). WAG expectations: a somewhat delayed straddle-in, maximum
  flexion at the bottom, and a continuous extension to a hollow end position with straight arms
  [2].
- **Toe-on (sole circle with the feet on the bar)**: from handstand or cast, place the feet
  (together, pike; or straddled) on the bar between or outside the hands. Circle backward in a deep
  pike with straight arms, the feet pressing the bar. The CoM stays close to the bar. Push off with
  the feet on the rising side and extend to handstand, to hang/swing, or to a release (toe-on to
  high bar, toe-on Tkatchev).
- **Errors**: bent arms, feet slipping, a slow pike, opening early.

### 3.5 Giants on UB
The backward giant on the high bar must pass over the low bar. Use the HB pattern, with a **hollow
tap** at the bottom. The arch is kept moderate so the feet clear the low bar. Expectations:
minimal pike and arm–trunk change, passing through an extended handstand [2]. Giants with ½ turns
start the turn before the handstand [2].

### 3.6 Transitions low ↔ high
- **Pak salto (high → low)**: a stretched back salto from the high bar that travels down to the low
  bar.
  1. Enter from a giant on the high bar, with an arch and then a tap at the bottom.
  2. Release early in the upswing. Fly in a straight to slightly hollow layout back salto toward
     the low bar (the CoM barely rises, ≈0.3–0.6 m, est.).
  3. Catch the low bar in hang and swing into a kip or cast.
  - Errors: piking or arching in flight, short of the low bar, bent arms at the catch.
  - *Check facing and side of release against video before keying.* The public sources found here
    gave no release angle; a 2025 developmental study exists but only its abstract was readable.
- **Shaposhnikova (low → high)**: a clear hip circle (or Stalder/toe-on variant) on the low bar.
  Instead of finishing in handstand, the gymnast releases on the rising part of the circle and flies
  to catch the high bar in hang. Variants add a ½ turn. Body piked or straight in flight.
  *Check the facing at the catch against video.*
- **Toe-on to high (and Maloney variants)**: a toe-on circle on the low bar, then release on the
  rising side and fly to catch the high bar in hang.
- **Kip/cast transitions**: a long-hang kip from the low bar to the high bar, and jumps to the
  high bar.

### 3.7 Releases (same bar)
- **Tkatchev (straddle, toe-on, Stalder, piked or stretched entries)**: the mechanics are as in
  §2.5. Research on UB shows that the longswing variant before it (arch, pike, straddle) changes the
  horizontal release velocity and angular momentum. The **arch** variant gave the largest values
  and suits harder combinations; the pike variant gave the lowest [9]. The hip generates most of
  the work, with a single large hip power burst peaking near horizontal on the upswing [10].
  "Inward" and "outward" Tkatchev (facing toward or away from the low bar) differ in hip range of
  motion (≈37° more in the inward version) and in shoulder action [10].
- **Jaeger, Deltchev, Gienger**: as in §2.5, adapted to the UB space. On UB, the Gienger or
  Jaeger is often followed by a giant.

### 3.8 Dismounts
- **Flyaway (layout, double tuck or pike, double layout, with twists)**: from a giant or a tap
  swing on the high bar. The pattern is the same as HB dismounts (§2.6).
  - Release angles at the 1994 World Championships: shoulder ≈144°, hip ≈148° [20].
  - Double tuck: release ≈60 ms after the CoM reaches its peak upward velocity. Late actions before
    release added ≈0.15 m to peak CoM height [21].
  - FIG: slightly hollow layout, whole body above the bar, pike over 45° or tuck = no value at that
    level [2].

### 3.9 Animation recipe — Uneven bars
- **Glide kip** (≈1.6 s):
  - Hang pike, hips behind the bar (0).
  - Glide, body extended, feet low (0.45).
  - Ankles at the bar (0.6).
  - Mid-kip: bar at the knees/thighs, arms straight, shoulders ≈90 (0.8).
  - Front support: hips on the bar, shoulders over (1.0).
- **Cast handstand** (≈1.0 s): front support (0) → small pike (0.2) → hollow rising, shoulders
  forward over the bar (0.6) → handstand (1.0).
- **Clear hip to handstand** (≈1.4 s): handstand or cast top (0) → falling back with hips close to
  the bar (0.3) → under the bar, hollow (0.5) → shoulders opening (0.75) → handstand (1.0).
- **Pak salto**: HB contact (giant downswing → tap → release pose) → flight (stretched, slightly
  hollow, back salto ≈ −300° to −360° depending on the catch geometry) → LB contact (hang, arms
  overhead).
- **Clearance rule**: on every HB circle, check that the feet pass above the LB. If they would hit,
  hollow or pike more at the bottom: real gymnasts do exactly that.

## 4. PARALLEL BARS (MAG)

### 4.1 Swing in support
- The **axis is the shoulders**. The body swings like a pendulum hanging from the shoulders, and the
  arms are straight and vertical [22].
- **Forward swing**: ends in a slight **dish with the hips above head height**.
- **Back swing**: must stay **hollow with the back leading** (not the heels). At higher levels it
  rises to momentary handstand [1].
- **Shoulder counter-movement**: the shoulders shift slightly backward on the front swing and
  forward on the back swing to keep the CoM over the hands (est.; standard coaching). The shoulders
  never sag ("long arms, shoulders pushed up").
- **Errors**: arching or heels leading on the back swing, a piked front swing, bent arms, sunken
  shoulders, swinging from the hips only.

### 4.2 Swing in upper-arm hang
- The upper arms lie on the rails, hands forward, elbows out. The axis is the shoulders, resting on
  the bars.
- **Forward swing** → **front uprise**: at the forward end, pike up (hips rise above the bars), then
  push down on the arms to rise to support. FIG: hips above the elbows at the end [1].
- **Back swing** → **back uprise**: at the back end (body above horizontal), press with straight
  arms to rise to support. FIG: back uprise above horizontal, arms straight at the end of the back
  swing [1].
- **Moy (layaway to upper-arm support)**: from support, lay away backward in a straight arm–trunk
  line, swing long under the bars in hang, and at the forward end pike and pull to upper-arm support
  with the body ≥45° above horizontal [1].

### 4.3 Glide kip on PB
Done at the end of the bars (or "long kip" inside the bars), with the same phases as UB §3.1.
Glide long and extended, ankles to the bar line, kip with straight arms, finish in support with the
hips at least at elbow height [1].

### 4.4 Stützkehre (swing with ½ turn in support)
- **Forward Stützkehre** (the usual one): on the front swing, as the hips rise above the bar line,
  shift the weight onto one arm. The body turns ½ about that arm while still rising, the free hand
  regrasps the opposite rail, and the swing continues backward to support or handstand. At higher
  levels the gymnast rises to handstand (Stützkehre to handstand).
- **Backward Stützkehre**: the same turn at the end of the back swing.
- Keys: the turn happens **at the top of the swing** (the slowest moment), with the hips high and
  the body hollow-dished. It ends with both hands back in support.
- **Errors**: turning low (hips below the bars), bent arms, arching, crashing into the bars after
  the turn.

### 4.5 Diamidov and Healy (one-arm pirouettes)
- **Diamidov**: a forward swing **up to handstand with a 1/1 turn on one arm**. The turn starts as
  the body rises past ≈45° above horizontal and finishes in handstand on both arms. The support arm
  is vertical and straight, and the body is straight/hollow with the head neutral.
- **Healy**: the turn happens **on the way down** from handstand. Swing forward down with a 1/1
  turn on one arm and finish in support or upper-arm support (FIG groups it as a turn after
  handstand). A kinematic comparison found clearly different angular velocity profiles between
  Diamidov and Healy, with the Healy's turn happening while the body accelerates downward [26].
- **Errors**: arm bending, a "late" turn that finishes off-vertical, a body piked or arched in the
  turn, hand placement not on the same rail line.

### 4.6 Peach basket (felge backward) and Belle family
- **Peach basket (basket to handstand)**:
  1. From support or handstand, lower backward and **pike in under the bars**: shoulders drop
     behind the hands, hips pass below the bars, legs close to the chest.
  2. Swing under the bars in a tight pike hang.
  3. On the rising side, **extend the hips and open the shoulders explosively** upward. Release
     and regrasp the rails and finish in handstand.
  - FIG lists Felge structures in its Technical Ability Testing (felge backward to support → to
    30°/45° → to handstand) [1].
  - Errors: a slow pike-in, the hips touching the bars, the regrasp before the body is vertical,
    bent arms in handstand.
- **Belle** (in this family): a basket that continues with a **salto backward before the regrasp**.
  It needs a higher extension and an earlier release than the plain basket. *Verify the exact
  current definition against the FIG Code of Points before animating*. Public sources were not
  conclusive.

### 4.7 Saltos in the bars (Morisue) and dismounts
- **Morisue**: the PB name for the **double salto backward** (in the FIG cross-apparatus name table
  next to Guczoghy on rings and Kovacs on HB) [3]. Done from a swing in support or from a giant,
  landing to upper-arm hang or support. *Confirm the start and end position in the current CoP.*
  - Pattern: a strong front swing in support, release with the hips high above the bars, double
    tuck with the CoM ≈0.8–1.0 m above the rails (est.), open early, catch.
- **Dismounts**:
  - Back salto tuck from the forward swing: release with the shoulders above the hands, the head
    forward (not thrown back), the CoM must rise, kick out before horizontal [1].
  - Front salto off the end from the back swing; double backs off the end; giant-swing dismounts.
  - Errors: head thrown back or leaning back for the salto (FIG lists this explicitly), no rise of
    the CoM, a late kick-out, grabbing the bar after landing [1].

### 4.8 Animation recipe — Parallel bars
- **Swing in support**: the hands are pinned on the rail and the shoulders move only a little.
  Keys:
  - Front end: body dished, hips above head height, shoulders a few cm behind the hands (0).
  - Bottom: body straight, vertical below the shoulders (0.25).
  - Back end: hollow, back leading, body above horizontal or to handstand (0.5).
  - Bottom (0.75) → front end (1.0).
  - ≈1.0–1.3 s per full swing (est.).
- **Stützkehre**: front-swing rise (0) → top with hips high, weight on one arm (0.35) → ½ turn on
  that arm (0.35–0.6) → free hand regrasps (0.6) → swing down backward (1.0). In 2D side view, show
  the turn with `facing` flipping at the moment the trunk passes edge-on.
- **Basket**: handstand (0) → shoulders behind the hands, pike-in (0.25) → bottom tight pike under
  the bars (0.45) → explosive extension (0.65) → hands off, flight to the rails (0.75) → regrasp,
  handstand (1.0).
- **Upper-arm swing**: the contact point is the upper arm (shoulders) on the rails, not the hands.

## 5. RINGS (MAG)

Rings are a free-hanging, moving apparatus. Coaches want **ring stillness at the end of every
swing to handstand**, and the CoM path should be as straight a vertical drop as possible rather
than "swinging the rings around". During circles, gymnasts regulate the effective pendulum length
(they shorten it by closing the shoulders and hips, and lengthen it by opening) [19].

### 5.1 Swings in hang (forward and back)
- Body long, arms straight, the rings held close to the body line.
- **Forward swing**: chest leads down, a slight arch before the bottom, then a **tap** (hip flexion
  and shoulder closing) so the feet lead up. The forward swing ends with the shoulders at least at
  ring height [1].
- **Back swing**: rising behind with the heels driving up and a slight arch at the end. The
  shoulders should reach at least ring height [1].
- The rings move forward and backward under the frame, opposite to the body's swing (est.).
  Animators may keep the hands pinned and accept a small simplification.
- **Errors**: swings below ring height, bent arms, rings "flying" away from the body, a piked swing.

### 5.2 Dislocates and inlocates
- **Dislocate** (on the forward swing): from a piked inverted hang or the end of a forward swing,
  **shoot the legs up and back** (hip extension), while the arms rotate out and wide sideways
  (the shoulders turn from in front of the head to behind it). Pass the top with a **straight body**
  and the shoulders at or above ring height. Swing down with the arms coming back in.
- **Inlocate** (on the back swing): at the end of the back swing, as the heels rise, **pike
  quickly** and let the shoulders rotate forward, arms wide, so the body rolls forward through
  inverted hang and continues into the forward swing. FIG: straight body, no stop in inverted hang,
  above ring height [1].
- Elite version: dislocates and inlocates **to handstand** or with saltos (FIG HB/R groups).
- **Errors**: a bent or piked body, no elevation, a stop in inverted hang, bent arms.

### 5.3 Kips and uprises (felges)
- **Kip (to L-sit or support)**: from a piked inverted hang, extend the hips up and forward at about
  45°. As the body rises, pull the rings down to the hips and turn the hands to support. End in an
  L-sit or support with straight arms, rings turned out [1].
- **Back uprise**: on the **back swing**, as the body rises behind, press down with straight arms
  (shoulder extension) so the shoulders rise above the rings. End in support or an L-sit above
  horizontal. Variants go to handstand, planche or swallow.
- **Front uprise**: on the **forward swing**, pull the rings down and back to the hips (shoulder
  extension with straight arms) and rise to support. The Honma family belongs here (below).
- **Felge forward/backward** (rolls): a slow rotation through inverted hang with straight arms to
  support, L, cross or handstand. FIG Technical Ability Testing has "Kips and Felges" structures [1].
- **Errors**: bent arms, resting on the straps, ending below horizontal, rings turned in.

### 5.4 Swing to handstand (backward and forward giants)
- **Backward giant (backward longswing) to handstand**:
  1. From handstand, **fall forward with a straight body**. The arms move **laterally outward**
     during the downswing, which reduces the shoulder force needed [15].
  2. Approaching the bottom: slight arch.
  3. Through the bottom: a fast **arch-to-pike** (hip and shoulder flexion, toes leading). The arms
     come back in toward the body line. This is the moment of maximum load.
  4. Rising: the body straightens and the arms press out and in to finish in a still handstand.
  - Measured cable tension exceeds 9 BW. A rigid straight body raises the peak shoulder force by
    ≈2.6 BW, and removing the lateral arm movement adds ≈0.7 BW [15].
  - Hip flexors mainly **stop excessive arch** on the downswing (the hip acts as an energy sink, not
    a source) [18].
  - Timing errors of ≈30 ms leave ≈7° of residual swing, and good technique can bring it below 1°
    [16]. A well-timed giant can even remove swing from a swinging handstand [17][18].
- **Forward giant to handstand**: back-first fall in a hollow shape, the same arm actions, and
  through the bottom a hollow/heel-drive.
- **Back uprise to handstand, Honma to handstand**: the swing rises directly to handstand with no
  full circle. The Honma swing to handstand is done from a pike or a stretched swing [3].
- **Errors**: swinging rings in handstand, bent arms, arched handstand, rings turned in, landing in
  handstand with the shoulders not over the hands.

### 5.5 Swing to strength positions
- **Principle**: arrive at the strength position with **zero swing**. The swing is used to rise,
  then the gymnast "**brakes**" with the arms into the hold. The position must be held 2 s (FIG).
  - Back uprise to planche or swallow (Maltese).
  - Front uprise to cross: the **Honma to cross** family, a forward swing pulling up with straight
    arms into the cross.
  - **Azarian**: a backward felge (roll) with straight arms to cross [3].
  - Nakayama: back lever to cross [3].
  - Swing to inverted cross.
- **Errors**: arriving with swing (rings moving), bent arms, a piked hold, the hold too high or too
  low, shoulders below the ring line in the cross.

### 5.6 Dismounts
- **Back salto stretched (basic)**: at the end of the forward swing, release **in front of the
  cables**, with the shoulders at least at ring height when inverted and a straight body throughout
  [1].
- **Double/triple saltos and twisting dismounts**: a strong backward giant or swing, release on the
  upswing, CoM rise ≈1.0–1.4 m (est.). Same flight rules as HB §2.6.
- **Errors**: late release (behind the cables), shoulders below the rings, piking in a layout.

### 5.7 Animation recipe — Rings
- **Swing**: hands pinned to `rings`.
  - Forward end: hollow, feet leading up, shoulders at ring height (0).
  - Bottom: straight to slight arch (0.25).
  - Back end: heels high, slight arch then hollow (0.5).
  - ≈1.1–1.4 s per half cycle (est.).
- **Dislocate**: piked inverted hang (0) → legs shoot up-back, arms wide (0.3) → straight body at
  the top with arms out to the side (0.5) → swing down with arms coming in (1.0). Body rotation
  ≈ +360° about the shoulders over ≈1 s (est.).
- **Back giant to handstand** (≈1.6 s):
  - Handstand (0).
  - Straight fall with arms widening (0.25).
  - Arch approaching the bottom (0.42).
  - Pike/tap at the bottom with arms in (0.5).
  - Hollow rise (0.75).
  - Handstand, still (1.0).
- **Swing to cross (Honma)**: forward swing (0) → pull with straight arms as the body rises (0.5) →
  arrive in the cross (front view or flat side view), then hold 2 s.
- **Rule**: no swing after any handstand or hold key. Rings and straps stay vertical in holds.

## 6. Cross-family checklist (what makes a coach say "not how it is done")
1. Arms bent in any swing element, except where the skill uses bent arms (chin-up casts at low
   levels).
2. The pike happening **before** the bottom instead of just after it (no tap).
3. An arched back on any back-leading swing (PB back swing, UB/HB forward giants on the downswing).
4. Circles finishing short of handstand or passing it in an arch.
5. Pirouettes started late, after the vertical.
6. Releases from the wrong point of the circle: a flyaway released at the bottom, or a Tkatchev
   released after the handstand.
7. Flight without the correct rotation sense (Tkatchev counter-rotation; Kovacs backward over the
   bar).
8. No landing absorption; landing with the CoM behind the feet.
9. Rings moving in a handstand or hold.
10. On UB, the feet passing through the low bar.
